# Meta ads on LinkedIn — digest and synthesis, 2026-10-08

**Asked by the owner:** "can you do a much more thorough research on linkedin? Is
there a way to scrape linkedin posts and analyse just like we analysed the 90
youtube videos?"

**What this is:** the LinkedIn counterpart of
`~/Downloads/meta yt lessons/SYNTHESIS-patterns-across-lessons.md`. Nine people's
recent posts were read through the owner's own Chrome, paraphrased, and compared
with that synthesis. Nothing here is a verbatim copy of a post.

**Status:** research only. Nothing built, no account setting changed, nobody
followed, nothing posted. **A second, deeper pass the same day is in §9 — read
it for the current counts and the updated synthesis.**
**The full synthesis across LinkedIn and 644 public blog and podcast items is
in `META-EXPERT-SYNTHESIS-2026-10-08.md`; start there.**

---

## 1. The answer in short

- **Yes, the YouTube method carries over**, with the unit changed from "one
  video" to "one person's last few weeks".
- **LinkedIn is the best free source found for one thing: what Meta changed this
  month.** It runs weeks ahead of YouTube. Two people carry most of that value.
- **It is weak for strategy depth.** Most of its durable advice repeats what the
  91 lessons already say (§4).
- **It does not fill any of the five gaps the YouTube synthesis lists** — India,
  low-priced experiences, invite-only events, WhatsApp after booking, Instagram
  in-app checkout. Still nothing on any of them.

## 6. Notes per person (paraphrased)

**Bram Van der Hallen** — the ten items are in
`META-FREE-LEARNING-SOURCES-2026-10-06.md` §9. Older posts added: per-ad
audience suggestions (his most-liked, 754 reactions), the Activity History
outcome column, the merged lift setup, and his first sighting of placement
removal about five weeks ago.

**Jon Loomer**
- Struggling for sales volume: start simple. Optimise for the purchase,
  consolidate budget, and leave settings alone unless there is a clear problem.
- Local businesses: small audience and small budget are the constraint. If you
  can optimise for a purchase or lead, do that; you are ahead of most.
- Optimising for a custom conversion (one product's sale) is not wrong and stops
  inflated results, with trade-offs.
- Sequenced Events are appearing; he has found no official documentation yet.
- Landing Page Experience, Automatic Events, and the new multi-media ad builder
  (buggy; can be switched off) each have a blog post.
- Blog: twelve principles for building Meta ads; how to approach control now.
- Strongly critical of the paid plans and the organic link limit.

**Barry Hott**
- 50 ads in one campaign: with limited conversions he expects fewer ad sets to
  do better; with plenty, no real difference. Good ads beat any structure.
- The setting he finds missing in accounts every week: audience segment
  breakdowns, with the customer lists behind them updating automatically.
- He almost never reads CPM, CPC, CTR or conversion rate alone. In his example
  the three best costs per sale had the three worst costs per click.
- Broad targeting with no exclusions drifts toward warm people, and creative
  teams then copy what "works" on warm people. He says this matters above about
  $50k a month.
- Most common audit mistake at any budget: pausing ads on ad-level cost per sale.

**Maurice Rahmey**
- The timeline of controls Meta has removed (in §3).
- A lead is a form fill, not a booking or a show-up; cheaper leads can mean
  worse ones. Revenue is lost between lead and attendance more than in the ad.
- Cost per lead is a range by offer and market, not an industry average.
- For brands with physical locations: plan one shoot as a shot list, cut it into
  ten or more ads, and vary by location.

**Olivia Kory**
- Do not reuse one incrementality factor in the peak season: the true return
  barely moves, what moves is how much each platform over-claims.
- Treats the "Close Enough?" study as required reading — the same paper as
  source 7 in the sources doc.
- Start with tests inside a channel, not a whole-Meta switch-off; match test
  length to the question; a "bad" result is useful if acted on.

**Eric Seufert** — reads Meta's ranking research and the rise of Meta's AI
agent; argues shopping agents face resistance from retailers. Direction only.

**Andrew Foxwell** — pointers to free blog posts: ten questions for auditing
your own account; 2026 agency pricing from 200+ agencies.

## 8. How to repeat it, and the limit

**Method:** Claude in Chrome → `linkedin.com/in/<address>/recent-activity/all/`
→ scroll → keep posts that mention ads → paraphrase. Addresses that work:
`bramvanderhallen`, `jonloomer`, `binghott`, `mrahmey`, `olivia-kory-50230812`,
`ericseufert`, `andrew-foxwell-2a533b7`. Guessed addresses fail silently with an
empty page; look the person up in LinkedIn's people search instead.

**The limit, and why:** LinkedIn's terms forbid automated collection, and this
runs on the owner's real account. Nine feeds read slowly in one sitting looks
like browsing. Keep to about a dozen feeds a sitting; no third-party scraper
that holds the login; no daily job.

**A route with no account at stake:** Loomer's blog and podcast, the Foxwell
blog and the Haus blog are public sites carrying the same material. Bram also
has a blog (bramsocial.com) — not checked whether it carries the same updates.
A repeating digest should read those sites and use LinkedIn only for the people
who post there first: Bram, Hott, Rahmey, Kory.

### 9.5 More things that touch chapter அ — to check, not decisions

Added to the seven in §5. None checked against the account.

8. **"Upgraded Pixel" setting.** Our Pixel was deliberately locked down. Bram's
   screenshot shows the new setting switched on by default with a June date. The
   handoff's Pixel-settings-file read is the place to look.
9. **Custom attribution for Purchase.** We already hold the true booking and its

    Suite can now hold it to view-only, which matches the read-only stance the
    handoff already takes.

    real spend and real tickets does not depend on Meta behaving.

13. **Automatic reply to ad comments.** A possible answer to the recorded worry
    that undecided visitors cannot find the site again. Not explored.

    years. Not checked what ours are set to.
