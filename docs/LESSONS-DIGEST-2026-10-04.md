**For Krutesh · Completed 8 October 2026 · Offline, read-only evidence review**

## 1. The digest on one page

**The strongest fit is a clear local offer, a small set of genuinely different messages, a matching website journey and decisions based on ticket economics. The lessons do not establish one winning campaign structure, scaling percentage or testing threshold for our business.**

**Coverage: 91 of 91 lessons read**, published 18 April 2024–29 September 2026. They have 48 literal channel names, not 48 fully independent experts: some channels share a presenter, and interviews repeat other people's views. Repeated videos do not receive repeated votes.

### Ten strongest patterns

The first seven meet this job's four-distinct-channel agreement rule. The last three are useful **views**, not consensus. Counts are verified named support, not a survey of all advertisers.

1. **Work from customer research, not invention — 4 channels.** Timpano, Theriot, Cordeddu, Diaz. [CF1]

### The five splits that matter most here

| Split | Best-fitting side for this business — manager judgement |
|---|---|
| Simple delivery or elaborate test/scale structures? | One offer in a concentrated setup fits the directly low-budget example. Extra cells must answer a real question, not imitate a large account. |
| Ask for Purchase or an earlier action? | Purchase fits our visible website checkout. Earlier actions are a conditional test, not purchases by another name. |
| Let delivery choose ads or force equal exposure? | For efficient selling, concentrated delivery fits. For learning about every idea, an unserved ad remains untested. The lessons do not settle the best fair-test design at ₹150/day. |
| Video only or clear static creative too? | Choose the format needed to explain the experience. The event lessons explicitly allow flyers and mixed formats. |
| Fixed 20% scaling or context-led changes? | Economics, observed results and seats decide. Our 20%-every-two-days rule is **our policy**, not expert consensus or a guarantee. |

For Founders Meet, the plan's **₹100 available per booking, ₹150/day and ₹2,000 example event limit** mean about **13.33 days**, not three weeks, at that daily pace. These are arithmetic consequences, not a request to spend.

**Your settled decisions stay settled:** Instagram-only, Purchase-first, /lifestyle, full ticket value reported at advance payment, and the recorded audience/placement choices. This report supplies evidence beside them; it does not reopen them.

Small samples widen uncertainty. That is a caveat, not a verdict that measurement or advertising is pointless. No launch date, spending increase, code change or deployment is proposed.

## 2. Glossary

| Term | Plain meaning in this report |
|---|---|
| Campaign / ad set / ad | The overall advertising objective; a delivery group; the actual message and media. |
| CBO / campaign budget | One pot allocated across ad sets. |
| ABO / ad-set budget | A separate budget assigned to each ad set. One ad per set does not by itself mean ABO. |
| Creative / concept / angle / persona | The message and media; an idea; a reason to buy; the person or situation addressed. |
| Hook / CTA | The opening that attracts attention; the call to action. |
| Andromeda | The newer Meta ad-selection system discussed in the lessons. Practitioner explanations are not verified platform specifications. |
| Broad / interest / lookalike | Few audience suggestions; named interests; an audience resembling a source group. |
| Warm / cold / retargeting | Already familiar with us; unfamiliar; advertising to previously engaged people. Broad warm is not cold broad. |
| Placement / Audience Network | A location where an ad appears; Meta's external app/site placement network. |
| Purchase / optimisation / proxy | The purchase event; the action delivery seeks; an earlier action used as a substitute signal. |
| Learning limited | A delivery-status label discussed by presenters; not a financial result. |
| Highest volume / cost goal / bid cap | Seek many results; target an average result cost; limit an auction bid. None guarantees each booking's cost. |
| CPA / CAC | Cost per selected action / customer acquired. A lead or checkout start is not a paid booking. |
| KPI | The particular outcome target being used. |
| ROAS / incremental ROAS | Credited revenue divided by ad spend / estimated additional revenue divided by spend. Neither is cash profit. |
| AOV / LTV / contribution | Average order value / lifetime customer value / money remaining after relevant costs. |
| CPM / CPC / CTR | Cost per thousand impressions / per click / clicks as a share of impressions. Specify which clicks. |
| Frequency | Average impressions per reached person, for a specified audience and time window. |
| Attribution / window | A rule assigning credit to an interaction / the period in which credit can be given. |
| Engaged-view / view / click credit | Credit after eligible video engagement / seeing an ad / clicking. The report uses the saved plan's terminology. |
| Incrementality / holdout | What happened because of ads / a comparison group withheld from exposure. |
| Pixel / CAPI / EMQ | Browser event reporting / server conversion reporting / Event Match Quality score. |
| Funnel / landing page | The steps from seeing the offer to buying / the first destination after clicking. |
| A/B test | A comparison designed to isolate a difference. Merely running different ads is not necessarily a controlled test. |
| UGC / DTC / CRM | Creator-style content / direct-to-consumer commerce / customer relationship records. |
| T3 / T2 second-hand / CONFIRMED | Practitioner opinion / a paraphrased account case with spend, result and period / source wording and count verified, not causal truth proven. |

## 3. Playbook for any low-budget, self-serve event

These are **applications of the verified lessons**, not results from a Chennai ticket-ad experiment. “Agreed” means four distinct supporting channels with no unresolved opposite claim at that narrow level; “thin” means fewer. Bracketed claim codes connect to the evidence register and source appendix.

| Decision, in order | Reusable advice and fit | Support / confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Offer and price | Work out money available for acquiring an attendee after relevant costs. Do not borrow a dollar ticket floor. Do not fund today's loss from imagined repeat attendance. | OF1:4, agreed; OF2:2, thin; EV8:2, contextual split |
| Research | Use real reasons, doubts and language from the intended attendee. An interesting competitor ad is not proof its economics transfer. | CF1:4, agreed |
| Message | State what happens, where and when. Make the experience clear; choose different reasons to attend rather than cosmetic duplicates. | EV11:4 and CR1:4, agreed |
| Format and quantity | Use video when the experience needs showing; a simple image can suit an already-understood event. No verified mathematical three-ad maximum exists. | EV12:4 across positions, contested; TH3:3, varied |
| Destination | Continue the same promise on the website. A separate page is not automatically necessary. | LA1:4, agreed; EV14:2, contextual |
| Structure | Keep one-offer delivery understandable. Heath's very-low-budget example favours one campaign, one set and minimal fragmentation; large-account testing architectures disagree. | TE4:1, thin; ST1–4: contested |
| Money and bidding | Keep the business's total-money limit explicit. Automatic bidding fits unproven creative; manual caps are not a way to guarantee a cheap ticket. | OF1:4, agreed; BI1–4: named views |
| Outcome | With visible checkout, ask for Purchase. Treat any earlier-event experiment as an experiment judged by actual bookings. | EV1:2, thin; OP1:2, contested |
| Audience | Start with feasible travel geography, not an imported radius. Broad delivery is compatible with hard local relevance; the lessons do not declare every lookalike obsolete. | TA1–4:1–2 per claim, thin |
| Timing | Reconcile daily pace, total budget and time to the fixed date. Event presenters allow weeks, but no universal start date follows. | EV5:3, thin; arithmetic in §7 |
| Evaluation | Put booking economics first; diagnose clicks, destination and checkout to locate the constraint. An ad that received no spend has not been fairly tested. | RR1:4, agreed; RR3:3, thin; ST3:1 |
| Changes | Separate a stop-loss, a review window and a winner-graduation rule. Do not wait for ten purchases before every possible intervention. | TH1–5, varied; TE1:2 |
| Final days | Use truthful deadline or seat information. Some event strategies concentrate on warm audiences; that does not require stopping all cold delivery in every event. | EV7:2, thin; RT2:1 event exception |
| Next event | Preserve results and attendee-contact opportunities for learning and future attendance. Do not promise that the next event will fill without ads. | RR1:4 principle; EV13:1, thin |

### What the most important splits mean in practice

**Structure and testing.** A system can be good at spending efficiently and poor at answering whether every neglected ad could work. Iles makes that distinction rather than rejecting CBO altogether. Our concentrated design is a reasonable fit inference, supported most directly by Heath's approximately $3/day example—not proven by every large-account consolidation video.

**Optimisation.** Our checkout is observable, unlike Southworth's venue-owned untrackable ticket link. That makes Purchase the closer event example. Earlier-event tests remain documented alternatives, not automatic instructions when a learning label appears.

**Creative.** New concepts and winner variations are not interchangeable units. An organic winner is evidence from a different delivery setting. The lessons support using distinct, relevant ideas; they do not give a universal conversion rate from organic success to paid success.

**Scaling.** Counting Theriot's two videos as two experts would exaggerate agreement. Adding Blue Sense and Cordeddu brings the named proposing set to four, but the two clear opponents still prevent calling it consensus. Strand's weekly reassessment is not Theriot's daily step.

**Retargeting.** Blue Sense's two “90%” statements concern different objects: most brands should not collapse everything into one ad set [L37:118], while most accounts do not need a separate site-visitor campaign [L37:184]. Both can be true in his framework. Charley T's “broad” retargeting still means people who already know the brand.
